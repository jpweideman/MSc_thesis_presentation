% Speaking notes
% We propose two models that combine a posterior over network weights with a Dirichlet distribution over class probabilities.
% Both represent aleatoric, distributional and epistemic uncertainty.
% Our analysis shows how total concentration controls the aleatoric and distributional allocation at fixed mean vectors.
% Single labels cannot inform this concentration. The categorical model therefore states an explicit assumption through its Gamma prior.
% Repeated labels change what the likelihood can inform. Their class counts can inform concentration and hence the allocation.
% The Dirichlet multinomial model also improves held out count prediction compared with the multinomial Bayesian baseline in our CIFAR-10H experiments.
% This predictive result does not establish better decisions from the uncertainty decomposition.
% Together, these contributions motivate collecting and retaining repeated labels for uncertainty-aware classification.
\begin{frame}{Main Contributions}
\label{frm:conclusion}
\label{frm:discussion}
\begin{itemize}
\setlength{\itemsep}{4mm}
\item Cat-eBNN and DM-eBNN represent \textbf{all three uncertainty terms}.
\item At fixed means, \textbf{total concentration} controls the aleatoric--distributional allocation.
\begin{itemize}
\item Single labels cannot inform it. Requires prior.
\item Repeated labels can inform it.
\end{itemize}
\item DM-eBNN improves \textbf{held out count prediction} over M-BNN on CIFAR-10H.
\item These findings motivate \textbf{collecting and retaining repeated labels} for uncertainty-aware classification.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
